# Performance principles

**English** · [한국어](performance-principles.ko.md)

We study drumming instruction and animation research, then design and test our own motion rules. A readable performance preserves the music, fits the body and makes the intended technique visible.

- Preserve original notes, times and velocities. Optional visual expression does not alter the sound.
- Fit seat and contacts using measured proportions and the same process for each avatar.
- Keep necessary preparation, contact, rebound and balance even in a restrained performance.
- Treat grip, sticking and pedal support as technique choices, separate from expressive gesture.
- Measure actual attached tools, posture and clearance. Accurate contact alone is insufficient when a wrist is unnatural or the body intersects the kit.

The [instruction register](../reference/sources/performance-instruction.md) credits Keiichi Nagata, Riley Warren and Dr. Dave Gerhart. [Research references](../reference/sources/research.md) cover motion generation, tools and data. Teaching examples are contextual starting points, not universal joint limits.

The detailed [performance and expression design](../../specs/architecture/performance-expression.md) explains how these principles become technique profiles, contact/support rules and a proposed expression amount from 0 to 100. That control is planned, not implemented or a measured emotion scale. Heel-down/heel-up should remain separate technique choices; current ankle motion is an approximation.

Time and clearance constrain optional gestures. A large accent must leave room for the next action, and a bar's first beat is not automatically a phrase climax. Adding an extra crash hit changes the arrangement and requires an explicit edit rather than a visual-expression setting.

See [feature availability](../reference/availability.md) for existing controls, and [calibration design](../../specs/architecture/calibration.md) for the body/kit fit that precedes more expressive movement.
